Client rebuild · Roofing · Muskoka

From dated to dominant.

A veteran roofing crew was carrying a website from another era: grey panels, broken image icons, a template shrug. The rebuild leads with what they actually have: standing-seam metalwork over the lakes, photographed the way it deserves. It runs the playbook from our roofing website design guide, applied to a real crew's real work.

  • The crew's own install photography as the full-bleed hero
  • Metal and flat roofing up front, where the owners asked for the focus
  • One call button, pinned to every scroll depth

Client rebuild · Law firm · Toronto

Authority, quietly.

The firm's old site was a template, and it read like one: glowing buttons over a grid, closer to a startup than to counsel. The rebuild is an editorial site in black and gold, with hairline rules instead of cards and the restraint you would expect from a lawyer.

  • A page for every practice area, each one answering first
  • A guided intake that sorts each inquiry before it reaches the firm
  • Text-size controls and a light and dark mode built in

Client rebuild · Cleaning · Waterloo Region

Same company, new market.

The business moved from Tampa to Waterloo Region, Milton and Guelph, and its old site was still selling premium Tampa living on a stock photo. The rebuild is cool morning light and brand navy, because clean is not beige.

  • Starting prices published on the page, before HST
  • A quote request that confirms the price in writing before booking
  • Every area served, named on its own page

Client build · Hypnotist · Brampton & the GTA

A comic book with a phone number.

A stage hypnotist with a big personality and no live website. So the site is a comic about him: the hero is the cover, every section is a panel, and the footer is the back cover. The joke is fully committed, and the phone number is impossible to miss.

  • A comic-cover hero in saturated flat primaries
  • Every section drawn as its own panel
  • Booking and a tap-to-call number on every screen

Client build · Training charity · Toronto

Light only shows in the dark.

A registered charity in Weston running free training programs in several languages. The idea comes from the lighthouse in its logo: paper sections to read, and dark sections where the amber beam switches on to show you something.

  • Free programs lead every page; paid services come last
  • Checked against Google's Ad Grants website rules
  • A free-session booking button on every screen

A showroom for the service call.

An owner-operated appliance, plumbing, gas and heating company. The owner asked for white, premium and calm, so the site works like a showroom: framed jobsite photography, light type, and brand red saved for the one thing that matters, the call button.

Client build · Hair salon · Keremeos, BC

Forty years, finally online.

A two-chair salon with four decades behind it and no web presence at all. The build keeps the room's character, the slate-blue walls, the wood, the station clock, and puts a working phone number a thumb's reach from every screen.

  • A palette pulled from the salon's own walls
  • Vintage trade-sign typography

Client build · Food bank · Toronto

Say it's your first time.

A community food bank in Weston, where the most important thing on the page is when the doors are open. Hours and the address come first, in plain words, right beside the client's own photo of the front door.

(03) Straight answers

Asked, answered.

Can my business get a build like these?

That's the entire offer: a custom site at this standard from $499, live in 48 hours once we have your content and payment. The price is published, and what moves it is scope: page count, whether we write the copy, extras like booking or e-commerce; the services page lays it out plainly. Every build is designed from scratch for the business, fast on a phone, and yours to keep outright at handover. The process page walks through exactly how the two days run.

How long would a build like this take?

48 hours from the moment your content and payment are both in. Before that, there's one short fit call where we agree on the scope and put the price in writing. If you need a week to gather your photos and text, the clock simply waits.

Can you rebuild a website I already have?

Yes, and the before-and-after sliders above show exactly what that looks like. A rebuild is often the fastest project there is, because the words and photos already exist. Send us the link and we'll tell you plainly what we'd keep and what we'd change.
