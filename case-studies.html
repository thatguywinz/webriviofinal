Both of these firms had a live website. Drag the line and decide for yourself what each one was saying about the work behind it.

Client rebuild · Roofing · Muskoka

From dated to dominant.

A veteran roofing crew was carrying a website from another era — grey panels, broken image icons, a template shrug. The rebuild leads with what they actually have: standing-seam metalwork over the lakes, photographed like it deserves. It runs the playbook from our roofing website design guide, applied to a real crew's real work.

  • Full-bleed jobsite photography as the hero, not stock
  • Every roof type gets its own page and its own proof
  • One phone number, pinned to every scroll depth
Before After

Client rebuild, nearing launch — the “before” is the site the firm was running when the project began; captured August 2026.

Demo rebuild · Law firm · North York

Authority, quietly.

The firm's old site wasn't broken. It was forgettable, glowing the way a thousand templates glow. The rebuild takes the opposite bet: an editorial page in black and gold, hairline rules instead of cards, and type that talks like counsel.

  • Zero shadows, zero rounded cards — restraint as the brand
  • Twelve practice areas, each with a page that answers first
  • A consultation path that never shouts

(02) Demo & client builds

Three niches, three signatures.

None of these businesses had a website — just booking links, map pins and camera rolls. Each build is designed from the business's real material — its photography, its menu, its room — because the same design twice isn't custom.

Demo build · Specialty café · North York · No website before

Measured to the gram.

A steel-and-black coffee bar that weighs every shot had nothing online but a map pin. The demo borrows the room's discipline — menu-board darkness, one warm line of light, and numerals set in mono because in this café, the numbers are the point.

  • A pinned brew-scale moment that counts the grams as you scroll
  • The menu typeset like the board behind the bar
  • Directions and hours answered before they're asked

Forty years, finally online.

A two-chair salon with four decades behind it and no web presence at all. The build keeps the room's character — the slate-blue walls, the wood, the station clock — and puts a working phone number a thumb's reach from every screen.

A poster you can order from.

A family phở stall in a farmers' market, invisible online. The demo prints its story like a Saigon poster — red-ink duotones on aged paper, postal stamps for frames — and lets the real bowls arrive in full colour, right before the phone number.

(03) Straight answers

Asked, answered.

Are these real client projects?

Two are — the builds marked Client: one live for a salon in BC, one rebuild nearing launch for a roofing firm. The rest are demo builds — complete, working websites we designed around real local businesses on our own initiative, to show the standard we ship at. Either way, we don't name a business here without the owner's permission.

Why don't you name the businesses in the demos?

Because they didn't commission the work. A demo shows our standard, not a client relationship, and we don't put a business's name on our site without the owner's permission. If an owner asks us to take a demo of their business down, we do.

Can my business get a build like these?

That's the entire offer: a custom site at this standard from $499, live in 48 hours once we have your content and payment. The price is flat and published, and what moves it is scope — page count, whether we write the copy, extras like booking or e-commerce; the services page lays it out plainly. Every build on this page, client or demo, was made the same way: designed from scratch for the business, fast on a phone, and yours to keep outright at handover. The process page walks through exactly how the two days run.
